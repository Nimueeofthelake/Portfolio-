01 — History of AI & ML

The Week 1 History lecture exemplifies the designer stance when ELIZA was discussed ELIZA provides an example of the designer stance in AI history. ELIZA was an early AI program made to emulate a human during conversations. ELIZA was created in 1964–1966 by Joseph Weizenbaum. ELIZA’s main system was very common for the time in that it relied on pattern-matching rules. The early models for AI were driven more by pattern-driven rules than by actually contextualizing information. However, ELIZA was the very first pioneer for modern-day chatbots.

Over time, AI has been refined from just the idea of an artificial being made by humans, like from Greek mythos or a golem. The legacy of modern AI starts with Alan Turing. Turing tested whether humans interacting with machines could tell if they were interacting with an AI or a human. Overall, AI was mainly used for either research or military purposes. It was only really in the 1980s, when computing became mainstream, that AI broke away from government exclusivity. As computing became more popular and hardware developed to be more powerful, AI evolved to become more efficient.
