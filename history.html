The designer's stance is essentially asking, “Can I build a device that can perform this task?” The philosopher’s stance asks the question that if the machine can perform this task ,does it have a mental capability? The designer sets out to make the machine itself, while the philosopher is asking if the machine has actual mental ability to solve this task. Both viewpoints would interpret the machine in two different ways. If the thermostat example is used the designer stance would desire to build a machine that can regulate temperature. The designer stance would see the thermostat as a system made to perform a certain desired task of keeping a room at a certain temperature. The philosopher would see that the thermostat appears to make the decision in either heating up the room or cooling it down, and question whether the machine has actual reasoning capability or just programming? What it means to answer the designer-stance version of the thermometer is that building the thermostat itself and performing its tasks successfully does not necessarily mean that the machine has mental capabilities in reasoning. In regard to how a moment from this week's Week 1 History lecture exemplifies the designer stance is when ELIZA was discussed. ELIZA provides an example of the designer stance in AI history. ELIZA was an early AI program made to emulate a human during conversations. ELIZA is connected to the designer stance because the researchers were concerned whether they could create a program that could emulate a human. Just because ELIZA can produce convincing human responses does not mean it understood the context or the conversation. As with the thermostat example, simply creating a machine to complete a task successfully does not mean that the machine possesses mental capabilities.
