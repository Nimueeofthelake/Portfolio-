01 — History of AI & ML

Over time, AI has been refined from just the idea of an artificial being made by humans, like from Greek mythos or a golem. The legacy of modern AI starts with Alan Turing. Turing tested whether humans interacting with machines could tell if they were interacting with an AI or a human. If the machine could convince the human they were talking to another human, the machine achieved its goal. This test paved the way for the chatbots of future decades. But just because the chatbot could fool a human does not mean it was actually intelligent or understood the conversation.

Early AI systems utilized the Logic Theorist and General Problem Solver, which essentially used math or an established rule/pattern to solve a certain problem. It was only until ELIZA in 1966 that an AI system used symbolic reasoning in its pattern matching to the user. Going forward, innovation in the AI field would be paused. It was only after computing became more commercially used by the public and computing power increased significantly that AI was brought out of its sleep. We can see AI gaining more popularity in the 2000s with internet-scale data that gave access to images, text, and other learning materials that learning-based models needed to work. It is from here that we see AlexNet using a deep neural network to correctly identify objects across millions of labeled photos. AlexNet has been foundational, as nearly every major AI system uses the AlexNet neural network as a basis for its system. From there, it was only in 2017 that we saw generative AI have its big moment, when researchers introduced a new kind of neural network that could weigh the relevance of every word in a passage at once, leading to a major development in language models.
