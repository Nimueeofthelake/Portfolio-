02 — Ethics & Society

The Predictive Policing Pilot (Criminal Justice) In this case, the police department of Fairmont utilizes an AI system called SafeZone. SafeZone's purpose is to analyze historical crime data to predict which neighborhoods are likely to see more criminal activity. Since the implementation of SafeZone, police patrols have been heavily funneled toward historically over-policed minority neighborhoods—not because of more crime happening there, but because it is predicted to happen there. Then, a police captain facing pressure to show results from the AI system begins pushing for more patrols into SafeZone’s flagged areas before the city council budget reviews to show that the AI system is working to secure next year’s funding. The ethical issue that SafeZone faces is the system's bias toward certain neighborhoods. This bias stems from historical data of certain minority neighborhoods used to predict criminal activity there. Since SafeZone has been trained on this historical data, which may itself be biased because police departments have historically made more arrests in minority neighborhoods due to officers' biases, the AI mimics that bias. The societal implications in Fairmont will result in more arrests in those minority neighborhoods compared to non-minority neighborhoods. This will also take away police resources from areas that may actually need interventions that the SafeZone AI has not flagged. The actions of the police captain also send a clear message to the community of Fairmont that the police are not to be trusted if they send out more patrols simply to secure their funding. This kind of situation in the real world would be catastrophic for police-community relations as public opinion of the police department falls after the targeting of Black communities in arrests and criminal policing. Realistically, if SafeZone were real, the AI system would target Black and other minority neighborhoods based on historically biased data and harm the public image of police officers.

The Essay Scorer (K-12 Education) In this case, the Springfield School District has started using ScoreWrite. The Scorewrite is marketed as removing “grader subjectivity” from writing assessments when grading assignments. What has happened in this case is that students writing in an English dialect or students learning English are given lower scores, even when their ideas and organization are just as strong as the essays that receive higher scores. ScoreWrite has also been used to evaluate teacher performance using trends in students' ScoreWrite results as an input, which the company that made ScoreWrite did not design it to do. There are two main ethical issues regarding the use of ScoreWrite. First, there is a clear bias in how the AI system grades essays. The training data ScoreWrite was given consisted of standard academic English, which did not include variations for English dialects or English language learners. This results in the AI having a bias to score standard English essays higher, while scoring essays by students who speak a dialect or are learning English lower because they do not follow the standard academic English it was trained on. Second, there is the misuse of student ScoreWrite results in teacher evaluations. ScoreWrite was never designed or tested as a measure of teaching quality. These ethical issues affect both students and teachers. Students learning English or using a dialect will receive lower scores on their essays, which could contribute to missing out on opportunities such as scholarships or university admissions compared to peers writing in standard academic English. Meanwhile, teachers whose students receive lower scores could risk job loss or funding cuts for programs like English language learning classes because the AI was misused to evaluate teaching effectiveness. Overall the bias and misuse of Scorewrite will negatively impact the students who do not write essays in standard academic-English and the teachers of those students. The SpringFeild School District needs to reexamine Scorewrite in its scoring bias and investigate the office responsible for teacher evaluations.
