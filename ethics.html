Fairness

The fairness of an AI system includes both how it was trained and how it performs, as well as how it interacts with people. A major issue regarding AI fairness is what data AI systems are trained on and how that data is used. We are at a stage of AI development where AI agents can mimic human performance in certain tasks, but that does not mean they are free from biases. AI biases stem from the original data on which the AI was trained. An example of this can be seen in Mobley v. Workday, which involves allegations of discrimination against Mobley based on his race, age, and disability. If this alleged discrimination did occur, one possible explanation is that the AI agent was trained on biased data that created certain preferences when evaluating potential new hires based on race, age, and disability. This would be an example of inherited human bias that the AI system carries into questions of fairness.

The Predictive Policing Pilot (Criminal Justice)

In this case, the police department of Fairmont utilizes an AI system called SafeZone. SafeZone's purpose is to analyze historical crime data to predict which neighborhoods are likely to experience more criminal activity. Since the implementation of SafeZone, police patrols have been heavily concentrated in historically over-policed minority neighborhoods—not because more crime is occurring there, but because the system predicts that it will happen there. A police captain, facing pressure to show results from the AI system, begins pushing for more patrols in SafeZone's flagged areas before the city council's budget review to demonstrate that the AI system is working and secure funding for the following year.

The ethical issue SafeZone faces is its bias toward certain neighborhoods. This bias stems from historical data about certain minority neighborhoods that is used to predict criminal activity. Since SafeZone has been trained on this historical data, which may itself be biased because police departments have historically made more arrests in minority neighborhoods due to officers' biases, the AI system mimics that bias. The societal implications in Fairmont will likely result in more arrests in those minority neighborhoods compared to non-minority neighborhoods. This will also take police resources away from areas that may actually need intervention but have not been flagged by SafeZone.

The police captain's actions also send a clear message to the community of Fairmont that the police are not to be trusted if they increase patrols simply to secure their funding. This kind of situation in the real world could be catastrophic for police-community relations, as public opinion of the police department may decline following the increased targeting of Black communities through arrests and criminal policing. Realistically, if SafeZone were real, the AI system could target Black and other minority neighborhoods based on historically biased data, ultimately harming the public image of police officers.

The Essay Scorer (K-12 Education)

In this case, the Springfield School District has started using ScoreWrite. ScoreWrite is marketed as a tool that removes "grader subjectivity" from writing assessments. However, what has happened is that students who write in an English dialect or are learning English receive lower scores, even when their ideas and organization are just as strong as those in essays that receive higher scores. ScoreWrite has also been used to evaluate teacher performance by using trends in students' ScoreWrite results as an input, even though the company that developed ScoreWrite did not design it for that purpose.

There are two main ethical issues regarding the use of ScoreWrite. First, there is a clear bias in how the AI system grades essays. The training data provided to ScoreWrite consisted of standard academic English and did not include variations for English dialects or English language learners. This results in the AI system having a bias toward scoring standard English essays higher while scoring essays written by students who speak a dialect or are learning English lower because their writing does not follow the standard academic English on which the system was trained.

Second, there is the misuse of student ScoreWrite results in teacher evaluations. ScoreWrite was never designed or tested as a measure of teaching quality. These ethical issues affect both students and teachers. Students learning English or using a dialect may receive lower scores on their essays, which could contribute to them missing out on opportunities such as scholarships or university admissions compared to their peers who write in standard academic English. Meanwhile, teachers whose students receive lower scores could risk losing their jobs or having funding cuts to programs such as English language learning classes because the AI system is being misused to evaluate teaching effectiveness.

Overall, the bias and misuse of ScoreWrite will negatively affect students who do not write essays in standard academic English and the teachers who educate those students. The Springfield School District needs to reexamine ScoreWrite for scoring bias and investigate the office responsible for teacher evaluations.

Positive Impacts

While there are many negative impacts, there are also benefits to AI. Some benefits include diagnostic improvements in healthcare that help identify diseases early in patients, assistance in preventing the creation of addictive drugs, and improved workflow management. AI is not entirely bad; its impact depends on how it is used.

Negative Impacts

Negative impacts of AI include the significant environmental toll associated with the water used by data centers. As referenced in the article "Does AI Use a Lot of Water? Inside AI's Hidden Resource Cost" by Jordan Hale, "an average ChatGPT query uses about 0.000085 gallons of water, roughly 0.32 ml or one-fifteenth of a teaspoon." What makes this problematic in the long term is that water sources may be drawn from local aquifers, potentially depleting them and affecting local water quality.

In Cheyenne, Wyoming, local waterways were tested and found to contain deadly bacteria in water that reportedly originated from a contractor's data center.
